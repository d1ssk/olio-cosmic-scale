# Earth surface texture

Credit: **NASA Earth Observatory — Blue Marble: Next Generation with Topography, July 2004**.

- [Source and download page](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/blue-marble-next-generation/base-topography/)
- [Original 5400×2700 JPEG](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/esd/eo/images/bmng/bmng-topography/july/world.topo.200407.3x5400x2700.jpg)
- [NASA Images and Media Usage Guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/)
- Retrieved 2026-09-10; resized proportionally to 2048×1024 JPEG with macOS `sips -Z 2048`. No geographic content was added or moved.

This is an equirectangular, cloud-free monthly surface composite with baked topographic shading, not a single photograph or live observation. NASA credit appears in both language versions of the scene. No NASA insignia or endorsement is used.

The geometry is an independently constructed sphere of radius 6,371 km, rounded from the [NASA/JPL mean-radius value](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/phys_par.html). Y is north, +X is longitude 0° at the equator, and −Z is longitude 90°E. It has no displaced terrain, atmosphere, oblateness, or simulated current sun position. The diameter-length reference bar is parallel to the polar axis at longitude 150°W, with its midpoint 1.15 mean radii from the center. This clearance is an adopted display convention. The bar and label use depth testing and are occluded by the globe when behind it.
